# Episode 1 Drawing Prompts

> Ready-to-use art prompts for *Fragment Away: Episode 1, PERFECT*. Use them as drawing briefs or as reference-image prompts. The look is a proposal. Keep every image separate so backgrounds, characters, UI, and cutscene poses can be recombined in Phaser.

## 1. Style block

Copy this style block before each environment or cutscene prompt:

> Soft hand-painted 2D graphic-novel illustration for a contemporary psychological indie drama. Natural proportions, simple readable shapes, selective pencil linework, muted cream, dusty blue, sage green, and soft amber, subtle paper or brush texture, gentle cinematic lighting, restrained emotion, side-on composition for a 2D walk-and-interact game, 16:9 landscape frame. The image should be atmospheric but easy to read at game scale. No pixel art, anime, 3D, photorealism, horror lighting, visual glitches, logos, watermarks, or readable text.

For **backgrounds**, add: “No people or characters. Keep the lower walking area clear. Leave important objects away from the extreme edges.”

For **character art**, add: “One character only, full body, side view, transparent background, no extra views, no text.” Choose each character's face, hair, and skin tone once, then reuse that exact design as a reference for every pose.

For **UI**, add: “Do not paint readable words. Leave message text and buttons blank so they can be real Phaser or HTML/CSS text.”

## 2. Background prompts

Draw these as separate images. The kitchen, bedroom, and street should have lighting variants made from the same base art, not entirely new paintings.

### Background 1: Mara's bedroom, morning

> A tidy but lived-in bedroom belonging to a young adult college student in Morrow, a modest contemporary college town. Fixed side-on view from near the doorway, with a bed along the left wall, a desk beneath a window on the right, and a clear floor path between the door, bed, and desk. Add a color-blocked calendar, a few academic certificates, a family photo, neatly stacked books, an exam study sheet, a packed college bag, and a smartphone. Outside the window, show an ordinary wet street and a faint view of the college buildings. The room is clean and controlled, not luxurious and not sad. Keep the phone screen and any writing blank. Leave room around the desk for Mara's sprite and interaction hotspots.

**Draw separately if needed:** Phone, certificate, study sheet, and family photo close-ups. Do not put legible writing in the background.

### Background 2: Mara's bedroom, night variant

Use the morning bedroom image as a reference. Keep the room layout, furniture, object positions, camera, and perspective exactly the same. Change only the lighting to a quiet blue-green night, with a small warm desk lamp and faint light from the phone. Keep it recognizable as the same room. No extra clutter and no horror effects.

### Background 3: Family kitchen, breakfast

> A warm, ordinary family kitchen in Morrow, illustrated from a fixed side-on camera. Show a small table with four chairs, coffee, a lunch container, a study sheet, a jacket over one chair, and a kitchen counter with a kettle. The room should feel cared for and comfortable, with aligned objects and a practical family routine. Include a doorway toward the hall. Leave clear standing spaces beside the table for Mara, Claire, and David. Do not include people or readable labels.

### Background 4: Same kitchen, dinner variant

Use the breakfast kitchen as the exact reference. Preserve the furniture and camera. Change the light to evening and swap only a few props: four plates, a water jug, a serving dish, and an extra place setting for the aunt. Keep the warmth genuine. The room should not look sinister. Make the arrangement neat enough to suggest family routine.

### Background 5: Home hallway and front door

> A modest family hallway in Morrow, viewed from a fixed side-on camera with a slight view toward the front door. Use orderly framed family photos, a narrow shoe rack, a small table with keys, and a coat hook with two everyday jackets. Keep the front door clearly visible and leave an unobstructed path from the bedroom side to the exit. Warm home light behind the camera, a cooler hint of night near the door. Symmetrical, tidy composition, no people, no text, no dramatic shadows.

### Background 6: Morrow street route, morning

> A short horizontal stretch of a contemporary small college town called Morrow, designed as a side-on 2D game background. Include ordinary houses, one modest bakery or corner shop, a bus shelter with a blank timetable, a crosswalk signal, a few street trees, and the college visible farther down the road. Make it feel lived-in and familiar, not wealthy, touristy, or fantasy-like. Use a damp morning with soft gray-blue light and a small warm shop window. Keep the sidewalk clear for a character sprite. No people, no cars blocking the path, no readable signs or logos.

If the route needs more space, split this into two connected street screens using the same colors and architecture. Do not draw a full town map.

### Background 7: Morrow street route, afternoon variant

Use the morning street art as the exact reference. Keep buildings, sidewalk, bus shelter, crosswalk, and camera in the same locations. Shift the light slightly warmer and dry the pavement. Change only small details, such as a shop awning being open or a bus farther away. The afternoon should still feel ordinary and quiet.

### Background 8: Morrow street, night variant

Use the same street reference and composition. Set it at night with a few lit windows, a pool of light by the bus stop, and soft blue-green shadows. Keep the path visible and safe to read. Do not add fog, threatening figures, horror lighting, or a dramatic skyline. Leave some open dark space around Mara for the ending shot and title overlay.

### Background 9: College hallway

> A clean, practical college corridor in Morrow, drawn as a fixed side-on 2D game scene. Include several classroom doors, one door with a visibly loose hinge, a simple noticeboard with blank flyers, a bench, ceiling lights, and a clear walking path. Use repeating straight lines and a tidy layout to echo Mara's controlled home. Keep posters as blank color blocks for later text. No people, no readable words, no logos.

### Background 10: Seminar classroom

> A modest college seminar room, fixed side-on camera, with a whiteboard or projector screen, a small instructor's desk, rows of chairs, a clock, and a wide open area where students can speak. Keep the room practical rather than grand. Leave the board empty or use abstract marks with no legible writing. Use restrained daylight and clean, orderly lines. No people.

### Background 11: College common room

> A small student common room in the same college, shown from a fixed side-on camera. Include a worn couch, low table, window, a few mismatched chairs, a noticeboard with blank flyers, and two mugs left behind. It should feel more relaxed and slightly less symmetrical than the classroom, but still like a believable everyday room. Keep a clear space for Mara and Jules to stand or sit. Use one modest warm color accent. No people, readable text, or logos.

## 3. Character prompts

The story has not locked skin tones, hair, or ethnicity. Choose those for each character yourself, then keep the design consistent. These prompts define posture, clothing direction, and mood without assigning an unapproved background.

### Mara, base sprite

> One full-body side-view character design of Mara Bennett, a young adult college student with natural proportions and an ordinary, approachable appearance. Simple everyday outfit: a plain sweater or cardigan, a shirt, straight-leg trousers or jeans, and practical shoes. Muted colors that fit Morrow. Her posture is neat and slightly held in, shoulders subtly tense, phone held close. Her expression is polite and observant, with tiredness visible only in small details. Avoid a model pose, exaggerated sadness, or a rebellious costume. Transparent background, one person, no text, no extra poses.

### Mara, expression and action poses

Use the approved Mara sprite as the image reference for every pose. Keep her face, hair, clothes, proportions, and colors identical.

1. **Recording a sound:** Mara standing still with her phone held out, focused on listening.
2. **Polite smile:** Small practiced smile, shoulders still, eyes attentive to a family member.
3. **Private laugh with Jules:** A brief natural laugh, shoulders relaxed, one hand half-covering a smile.
4. **Phone in bedroom:** Mara sitting on the edge of the bed, looking down at the lit phone, expression worn out but restrained.
5. **Packing:** Mara crouched beside a small open bag, deciding whether to take a personal item.
6. **At the front door:** Mara standing with the bag strap in one hand, paused before opening the door.

Each pose should be a separate drawing. Do not put all six poses into one sheet.

### Claire, base sprite

> One full-body side-view design of Claire Bennett, Mara's adult mother. Practical, neat clothes in warm muted colors. Her posture should feel attentive and caring, with small gestures such as offering a jacket, setting down food, or straightening Mara's collar. Her expression can carry concern without looking angry or villainous. Natural proportions, simple readable face, transparent background, one person only, no text.

### David, base sprite

> One full-body side-view design of David Bennett, Mara's adult father. Practical shirt or sweater, tidy but not formal. Give him an upright, focused posture and a few caring gestures, such as placing a study sheet by Mara's bag or pouring water at dinner. His face should read as earnest and achievement-focused, not cruel. Natural proportions, simple readable face, transparent background, one person only, no text.

### Jules, base sprite

> One full-body side-view design of Jules, a college classmate whose final identity and appearance are still open. Casual layered campus clothes, a folder or notebook, relaxed posture, and a small amused expression. Jules should look like an ordinary student, not a glamorous model or a perfect sidekick. Choose a clear silhouette and one small color accent that contrasts gently with Mara's muted outfit. Transparent background, one person only, no text.

### Professor, base sprite

> One full-body side-view design of a college professor in practical professional clothes. Give them an open posture, a paper or folder, and a calm expression that communicates genuine encouragement. They should not look like an antagonist. Natural proportions, restrained facial detail, transparent background, one person only, no text.

### Aunt, base sprite

> One full-body side-view design of Mara's aunt, an adult relative dressed neatly for a family dinner. Warm expression, composed posture, and a small welcoming gesture. She is proud of Mara and repeats the family's expectations without intending harm. Avoid a caricature of a judgmental relative. Natural proportions, transparent background, one person only, no text.

## 4. Cutscene prompts

Use the same backgrounds and approved character references from above. Each prompt describes one frame. Do not combine them into a collage. Text, messages, and the game title should be added in the game, not painted into the image.

### Cutscene 1: The Smile

**Frame 1, family dinner:**

> Wide side-on view of the Morrow family dining scene. Mara is smiling after her aunt says the family is proud of her. Claire reaches for a serving dish and David pours water. The family feels genuinely affectionate. Keep the frame tidy and balanced, with warm light and no threatening expressions. Use the approved character references and dinner background.

**Frame 2, hallway:**

> Side-on cinematic view of Mara walking away from the dining area toward the bedroom. Her practiced smile is still present, but beginning to loosen. Her family stays softly out of focus in the warmly lit room behind her. Keep the hallway straight and symmetrical. Use a restrained camera move, not a dramatic angle.

**Frame 3, bedroom:**

> Quiet medium close-up of Mara just after she closes her bedroom door. The room is still and familiar. Her smile has faded, but she is not crying. Show a small breath and a tired, uncertain expression. Cool night light on one side of her face and a little warm hall light disappearing at the door. No glitch effects, no text.

### Cutscene 2: The Stack

**Frame 1, phone:**

> Over-shoulder view of Mara sitting in her bedroom with her phone in hand. Several message bubbles are represented by blank, readable shapes only. Keep the text area empty because actual messages will be added as UI. The phone light falls softly on her fingers. The background is the same bedroom night image. No legible writing and no brand logo.

**Frame 2, phone face-down:**

> Close view of Mara's hand turning the phone face-down on the bed or desk. Her other hand rests still nearby. The light from the screen disappears. Quiet, simple composition, muted blue-green night palette, no dramatic shadow, no text.

### Cutscene 3: Out the Door

**Frame 1, the bag:**

> Mara crouches beside a small open bag in her bedroom. A charger, wallet, folded clothes, keys, and her phone are visible. Certificates and a family photo remain on the wall. She pauses with one hand near the bag, unsure, not crying. Use the established bedroom layout and soft night light.

**Frame 2, hallway:**

> Wide, centered view from inside the home as Mara walks through the quiet hallway toward the front door, carrying the small bag. Family photos line the wall. She is small in the frame. Keep the lighting dim but readable and the composition symmetrical.

**Frame 3, threshold:**

> View from just inside the house as Mara opens the front door and steps into the night in Morrow. The inside hallway is warm behind her; outside is cool and quiet. Show her mostly from behind, with the bag, and leave clean negative space above or beside her for a title added later. No readable text or dramatic storm.

## 5. Small props and UI

Most props can stay in the background painting. Draw a separate image only if the player needs to inspect, move, animate, or zoom into it.

- **Phone:** Plain generic smartphone, no brand, no readable screen text. The phone body can be an image; screen contents should be HTML/CSS or Phaser text.
- **Sound recordings:** Use simple waveform shapes and short file labels as real UI text. Do not bake the labels into a picture.
- **Study sheet and certificates:** Paper props with abstract lines only. Add actual writing later if it is readable in a close-up.
- **Bag and keys:** Simple, everyday objects with no brand marks.
- **Bus timetable and campus flyers:** Paint blank sign areas, then add words as game text.
- **Interaction markers:** Small outline icon or subtle highlight made in Phaser. These do not need separate painted art.

## 6. What to draw first

Do not try to draw everything at once. Start with the bedroom, then test the scene in Phaser with a simple Mara placeholder.

1. Bedroom background in daylight.
2. Mara's base side-view design and a plain placeholder walk cycle.
3. Bedroom night lighting variant.
4. Kitchen/dining background with morning and evening prop changes.
5. Morrow street route.
6. College hallway, seminar room, and common room.
7. Claire, David, Jules, Professor, and Aunt.
8. The three cutscene frames that need extra poses.

If the character scale, camera, and walking space do not work in the rough sketch, fix that before adding detail. The goal is a consistent playable scene, not a perfect painting on the first try.
